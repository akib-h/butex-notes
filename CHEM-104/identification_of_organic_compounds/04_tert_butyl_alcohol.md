# Tert-Butyl Alcohol ((CH₃)₃COH) — Identification

## Physical Properties

* Colourless liquid/low-melting solid with a camphor-like odour (melts near room temperature, m.p. ≈ 25 °C).
* B.P. = 82.4 °C.
* Tertiary alcohol — the carbon bearing –OH has no hydrogen attached, only three methyl groups.

## Tests

### (i) Iodoform Test

Sample + I₂ solution + NaOH, warm
→ **no** yellow precipitate

**Observation:** Negative — no iodoform forms.

**Inference:** No CH₃CH(OH)– grouping is possible; the carbinol carbon is fully substituted by three methyl groups and bears no hydrogen.

### (ii) Oxidation Test

Sample + K₂Cr₂O₇ + dil. H₂SO₄, warm
→ **no colour change**, no characteristic smell

**Observation:** Orange dichromate colour persists (does not turn green); no aldehyde/ketone smell develops.

**Inference:** Tertiary alcohols resist oxidation by chromic acid, because oxidation requires a hydrogen atom on the carbon bearing the –OH group, which tert-butyl alcohol does not have.

### (iii) Lucas Test

Sample + Lucas reagent (anhydrous ZnCl₂ in conc. HCl), shake
→ turbidity appears **instantly** (within seconds, no warming needed)

**Observation:** Immediate cloudiness/separation of an oily layer of tert-butyl chloride.

**Reaction:**

$$\ce{(CH3)3COH + HCl ->[ZnCl2] (CH3)3CCl + H2O}$$

**Inference:** Confirms a tertiary alcohol — the reaction proceeds via a stable tertiary carbocation (SN1 mechanism), so it is the fastest of the three alcohol classes.

## Comparison Table — All Four Alcohols

| Property | MeOH | EtOH | iPrOH | tBuOH |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Class | 1° (only 1 C) | 1° | 2° | 3° |
| Iodoform test | – | + | + | – |
| Lucas test | No reaction | No reaction (slow) | Cloudy in 5–10 min | Cloudy instantly |
| Oxidation product | Aldehyde (formaldehyde) | Aldehyde (acetaldehyde) | Ketone (acetone) | No oxidation |

**Why Lucas test separates them:** the reaction proceeds through a carbocation intermediate (SN1); carbocation stability follows 3° > 2° > 1°, so turbidity speed follows the same order.
