# Oxalic Acid (HOOC–COOH) — Identification

## Physical Properties

* Colourless, odourless white crystalline powder with a musky, acidic taste.
* Crystallises from water as the dihydrate (HOOC–COOH·2H₂O).
* M.P. of hydrated acid ≈ 101–101.5 °C.
* M.P. of anhydrous acid ≈ 189–189.5 °C.
* Soluble in water and ethanol; practically insoluble in ether.
* An active poison — forms insoluble calcium oxalate in the body, disrupting calcium metabolism.

## Tests

### (i) Oxidation Test (KMnO₄)

Oxalic acid + KMnO₄ + dilute H₂SO₄, heat to 60 °C
→ CO₂ + K₂SO₄ + MnSO₄ + H₂O

**Observation:** Purple colour of KMnO₄ disappears.

**Reaction:**

$$\ce{2KMnO4 + 5(COOH)2 + 3H2SO4 ->[60^\circ C] 10CO2 ^ + K2SO4 + 2MnSO4 + 8H2O}$$

### (ii) Action with Conc. H₂SO₄

Oxalic acid + conc. H₂SO₄, warm
→ CO₂ + CO + H₂O

**Observation:** Effervescence of a gas mixture (CO₂ + CO).

**Reaction:**

$$\ce{HOOC-COOH ->[conc. H2SO4][\Delta] CO2 ^ + CO ^ + H2O}$$

### (iii) CaCl₂ Test

Oxalic acid + CaCl₂ solution
→ white precipitate of calcium oxalate + HCl

**Observation:** White precipitate forms; **soluble in HCl but insoluble in acetic acid** — this solubility pattern is the confirmatory part of the test.

**Reaction:**

$$\ce{HOOC-COOH + CaCl2 -> CaC2O4 v + 2HCl}$$

## Comparison Note

Unlike formic acid, oxalic acid gives **no** Tollens'/Fehling's reaction — it has no aldehyde-like character, only a diacid structure. The CaCl₂ solubility test (soluble in HCl, insoluble in CH₃COOH) is the most specific confirmatory test for oxalate.
