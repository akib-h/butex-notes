# Benzoic Acid (C₆H₅COOH) — Identification

## Physical Properties

* White crystalline solid.
* M.P. = 122 °C.
* Sublimes readily on gentle heating.
* Sparingly soluble in cold water; soluble in hot water, ethanol, and ether.

## Tests

### (i) NaHCO₃ Test

C₆H₅COOH + NaHCO₃
→ C₆H₅COONa + CO₂ + H₂O

**Observation:** Effervescence of CO₂ gas.

**Reaction:**

$$\ce{C6H5COOH + NaHCO3 -> C6H5COONa + CO2 ^ + H2O}$$

### (ii) Solubility Test

Sample + cold water → insoluble/sparingly soluble.
Sample + NaOH or Na₂CO₃ solution → dissolves readily.

**Observation:** Poor solubility in cold water, but dissolves as the salt forms in alkali.

**Inference:** Weak aromatic acid; solubility behaviour distinguishes it from more water-soluble aliphatic acids.

### (iii) Esterification Test

C₆H₅COOH + MeOH or EtOH + conc. H₂SO₄, warm
→ methyl/ethyl benzoate + H₂O

**Observation:** Faint sweet, fragrant ester smell.

### (iv) Sublimation Test

Heat the solid sample gently in a china dish covered with an inverted funnel (cotton plug in the funnel stem).
→ white needle-like crystals collect on the cool underside of the funnel/cover.

**Observation:** White crystalline sublimate forms without melting first — a useful confirmatory physical test alongside melting point.

## Comparison Note

See [09_salicylic_acid.md](09_salicylic_acid.md) — salicylic acid gives the same NaHCO₃ effervescence but *additionally* gives a violet colour with neutral FeCl₃ (phenolic –OH), which benzoic acid does not.
