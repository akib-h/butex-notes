# Salicylic Acid (2-Hydroxybenzoic Acid) — Identification

## Physical Properties

* White crystalline solid with a sweetish, acrid taste.
* M.P. = 158–160 °C.
* Slightly soluble in cold water; more soluble in hot water, ethanol, ether.
* Has **two** functional groups: an aromatic –COOH and a phenolic –OH — so it must be confirmed by tests for both.

## Tests

### (i) NaHCO₃ Test (for –COOH)

Salicylic acid + NaHCO₃
→ effervescence of CO₂

**Observation:** Brisk effervescence, confirming the free carboxylic acid group.

### (ii) Neutral FeCl₃ Test (for phenolic –OH)

Salicylic acid solution + neutral FeCl₃
→ **violet colour**

**Observation:** Intense violet/purple colouration.

**Inference:** Confirms the phenolic –OH group; this is the test that distinguishes salicylic acid from plain benzoic acid.

### (iii) Esterification Test (Methyl Salicylate Formation)

Salicylic acid + methanol + conc. H₂SO₄, warm
→ methyl salicylate + H₂O

**Observation:** Strong wintergreen (oil-of-wintergreen) smell.

**Reaction:**

$$\ce{C6H4(OH)COOH + CH3OH ->[conc. H2SO4][\Delta] C6H4(OH)COOCH3 + H2O}$$

### (iv) Bromine Water Test

Salicylic acid + bromine water
→ white precipitate

**Observation:** White precipitate forms (ring bromination ortho/para to the activating –OH group).

**Inference:** Confirms a highly activated aromatic ring, consistent with a phenolic –OH substituent.
