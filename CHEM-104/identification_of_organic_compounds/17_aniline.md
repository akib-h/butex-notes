# Aniline (C₆H₅NH₂) — Identification

## Physical Properties

* Colourless oily liquid, turns brown on standing/exposure to air and light.
* B.P. = 184 °C.
* Slightly soluble in water; soluble in organic solvents.

## Tests

### (i) Carbylamine (Isocyanide) Test

Aniline + CHCl₃ + alcoholic KOH, warm gently
→ extremely offensive, foul smell

**Observation:** Sharp, unpleasant "rotten" odour of phenyl isocyanide.

**Reaction:**

$$\ce{C6H5NH2 + CHCl3 + 3KOH -> C6H5NC ^ + 3KCl + 3H2O}$$

**Inference:** Positive only for **primary** amines (aliphatic or aromatic).

### (ii) Bromine Water Test

Aniline + bromine water
→ white precipitate of 2,4,6-tribromoaniline

**Observation:** White precipitate forms immediately.

**Reaction:**

$$\ce{C6H5NH2 + 3Br2 -> C6H2Br3NH2 v + 3HBr}$$

### (iii) Diazotisation and Azo Coupling Test

Aniline + NaNO₂/HCl at 0–5 °C (diazotisation), then couple with an alkaline solution of β-naphthol
→ orange/red azo dye

**Observation:** Bright orange-red colouration/precipitate.

**Inference:** Confirms a primary aromatic amine. **Note:** the diazotisation step must be kept at 0–5 °C, or the diazonium salt decomposes and the test gives a false negative.

### (iv) Acetylation Test

Aniline + acetic anhydride, warm gently
→ acetanilide (solid, sharp melting point)

**Observation:** White crystalline solid separates on cooling.
