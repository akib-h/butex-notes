# Chlorobenzene (C₆H₅Cl) — Identification

## Physical Properties

* Colourless liquid with a faint almond-like smell.
* B.P. = 132 °C.
* Immiscible with water.

## Tests

### (i) Direct AgNO₃ Test

Chlorobenzene + AgNO₃ solution (cold, direct addition)
→ **no precipitate**

**Observation:** No white ppt. forms.

**Inference:** The C–Cl bond has partial double-bond character from conjugation with the aromatic ring, making chlorine essentially covalent and non-ionisable under mild conditions — unlike an alkyl chloride, which would give an immediate AgCl precipitate.

### (ii) Beilstein Test

Clean a copper wire by heating in a Bunsen flame until no colour shows, then dip it in the sample and reheat in the flame.
→ **green flame**

**Observation:** Bright green (sometimes blue-green) flame colouration.

**Inference:** Confirms the presence of a halogen (does not distinguish which halogen).

### (iii) Fusion (Lassaigne's Test) + AgNO₃

Fuse the sample with sodium metal to convert covalent Cl to ionic NaCl, extract with water, then add dilute HNO₃ and AgNO₃.
→ white precipitate of AgCl, soluble in NH₄OH

**Observation:** White curdy precipitate, dissolves back on adding ammonium hydroxide.

**Inference:** Confirms chlorine specifically, after first converting it to the ionic form via fusion.

## Comparison Note

The key teaching point: chlorobenzene fails the *direct* AgNO₃ test (unlike an alkyl halide) but passes both the Beilstein test and the fusion + AgNO₃ test, because those methods don't rely on direct ionisation of the C–Cl bond.
