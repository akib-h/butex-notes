# Urea (NH₂CONH₂) — Identification

## Physical Properties

* White crystalline solid, odourless.
* M.P. = 132–135 °C.
* Very soluble in water.

## Tests

### (i) Biuret Test

Heat urea gently (dry, in a test tube) until it melts and partially decomposes to biuret (loses NH₃), then cool, dissolve in water, add dilute NaOH and a drop of dilute CuSO₄.
→ **violet-pink colour**

**Observation:** Violet/pink colouration.

**Reaction:**

$$\ce{2NH2CONH2 ->[\Delta] NH2CONHCONH2 + NH3 ^}$$

(biuret formed above then chelates Cu²⁺ in alkaline solution to give the coloured complex)

**Inference:** This test specifically detects the biuret linkage, not urea directly — urea must be heated first to form biuret.

### (ii) NaOH + Heat Test

Urea + NaOH solution, warm/boil
→ pungent ammonia gas evolved

**Observation:** Sharp NH₃ smell; turns moist red litmus paper blue.

**Reaction:**

$$\ce{NH2CONH2 + 2NaOH -> Na2CO3 + 2NH3 ^}$$

### (iii) Xanthydrol Test

Urea + xanthydrol reagent (in acidic alcohol)
→ white crystalline precipitate of dixanthylurea

**Observation:** White crystalline solid separates out.

**Inference:** Highly specific confirmatory test for urea.

## Comparison Note

See [21_benzamide.md](21_benzamide.md)/[23_acetamide.md](23_acetamide.md) — all three release NH₃ on heating with NaOH, but only urea gives the violet biuret colour and the xanthydrol precipitate.
