# Benzamide (C₆H₅CONH₂) — Identification

## Physical Properties

* White crystalline solid with a faint odour.
* M.P. = 130 °C.

## Tests

### (i) NaOH + Heat Test

Benzamide + NaOH solution, warm/boil
→ ammonia gas evolved

**Observation:** Litmus paper (moist, red) turns blue; pungent NH₃ smell.

**Reaction:**

$$\ce{C6H5CONH2 + NaOH -> C6H5COONa + NH3 ^}$$

### (ii) Hydrolysis Test, then NaHCO₃ on the Product

Hydrolyse benzamide (boil with dilute acid or alkali), then acidify and test the isolated solid with NaHCO₃.
→ benzoic acid regenerated; effervescence with NaHCO₃

**Observation:** White solid (benzoic acid) precipitates on acidifying; gives CO₂ effervescence with NaHCO₃, confirming the –COOH.

### (iii) Hofmann Bromamide Degradation

Benzamide + Br₂ + NaOH
→ aniline forms

**Observation:** Product gives a positive carbylamine test (foul smell) — confirms the amide has degraded one carbon down to the corresponding primary amine.

**Inference:** This reaction is a classic confirmatory step distinguishing an amide from a nitrile, since only an amide undergoes this specific degradation.

## Comparison Note

See [20_urea.md](20_urea.md) — benzamide releases NH₃ on NaOH just like urea, but does **not** give the biuret violet colour, distinguishing it from urea.
