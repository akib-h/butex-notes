# Acetanilide (C₆H₅NHCOCH₃) — Identification

## Physical Properties

* White crystalline solid, odourless.
* M.P. = 114 °C.

## Tests

### (i) NaOH + Heat Test

Acetanilide + NaOH solution, warm/boil
→ **weak/slow** ammonia evolution (unlike a simple amide)

**Observation:** Little to no immediate NH₃ smell under mild warming.

**Inference:** The nitrogen atom is substituted by a phenyl group (–NHCOCH₃, not –CONH₂), so simple base treatment does not readily liberate free NH₃; full hydrolysis is required.

### (ii) Acid/Base Hydrolysis Test

Boil acetanilide with dilute HCl (or NaOH) for an extended period.
→ aniline + acetic acid

**Observation:** Two products form — an oily/basic layer (aniline) and, after neutralising, a vinegar-like smell (acetic acid).

**Reaction:**

$$\ce{C6H5NHCOCH3 + H2O ->[H+ or OH-][\Delta] C6H5NH2 + CH3COOH}$$

### (iii) Confirmatory Test on the Aniline Fraction

Take the hydrolysed aniline layer and run the carbylamine test (+ CHCl₃ + alcoholic KOH) or the bromine water test.
→ foul carbylamine smell, or white tribromoaniline precipitate

**Observation:** Confirms the aniline core released by hydrolysis.

## Comparison Note

See [17_aniline.md](17_aniline.md) — acetanilide is essentially "protected" aniline; hydrolysing it regenerates the free amine, which then gives all the standard aniline tests.
